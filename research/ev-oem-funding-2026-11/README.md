# How EV OEMs Raised Funding in Their Early Stages

**Research date: 11 November 2026.** Sources verified against primary publications — company announcements, gazette notifications, government portals, SEC filings, and reputable business press (ET, Moneycontrol, Inc42, Entrackr, YourStory, TechCrunch, Tech.eu, Reuters).

Every figure below is tied to a published source. Undisclosed fields are marked **"not disclosed"** rather than estimated.

---

## The headline finding

**In EV, the "seed" round doesn't look like a seed round, and the reason investors give is a structural trap.**

Blume Ventures' Arpit Agarwal, writing on India's two-wheeler EV market:

> "a young startup is unable to sell because it hasn't proven its reliability, but it also won't become reliable unless it sells large quantities of its vehicle."

A VC cannot value a vehicle without usage data, and usage data requires volume. Every structure below is an attempt to solve that chicken-and-egg problem.

Agarwal adds the test that decides the round:

> "building a successful business in the EV space is not just about creating a good product. You also need to make sure that the vehicle is manufactured at the right cost."

And the sobering caveat from the same thesis: **Ather's outcome does not transfer.** It was first-mover with a decade of head start. *"If OEMs have to start businesses today, they either need headstarts that Ather Energy or Ultraviolette had... or a huge capital investment like Ola. If none is in place, it will be a tough battle."*

## 3. Six patterns that hold across both geographies

### Pattern 1 — Strategic/corporate capital substitutes for VC money
Bajaj Auto wrote Yulu's Series A. TVS Motor led Ultraviolette's round and ended at 33.95%. Bharat Forge already owned 52.46% of Tork before Maxis came in. Qatar's Al-Abdulla Group wrote a single $50M cheque to Kabira. Greaves Cotton acquired Ampere onto its balance sheet.

Globally: **Geely** anchored Zeekr's first external $500M; **CATL and Intel Capital** bought in alongside. **Alibaba and Foxconn** took XPeng's Series B. **Evergrande** put $2.0B into Faraday Future at $4.4B. **Xiaomi** led XPeng's $400M Series C. **Repsol and Caixa Capital Risc** co-led Silence's €8.6M Series A, with the Catalan government participating.

**Why:** an OEM can underwrite tooling access, supply chain and distribution in a way a financial VC cannot — and that capital carries different risk.

### Pattern 2 — Round labels are unreliable
Ultraviolette's $15M was labelled a **Series C** while still setting up its facility. Simple Energy's pre-series round oversubscribed from $15M to $21M, then needed a ~$20M bridge. Hero Electric's first institutional cheque sat on the books **two and a half years** before the next tranche. Lucid's $1.5M Series A (Jan 2010) was followed by a $7.1M Series B *in December of the same year*.

**Benchmark against money and instrument, not the label on the press release.**

### Pattern 3 — Equity–debt blends are the norm
Oben's ₹40 cr was explicitly *"in equity and debt"* — **Stride Ventures and IREDA** in the debt tranche. Bounce's $20M was explicitly a *"lifeline"*. Simple Energy needed a bridge. Lucid's ladder opens with $150K of debt from GE Ventures *before* its Series A. Faraday Future's ladder opens with $13.8M of debt. Arrival later raised $500M and $50M in post-IPO debt. Cake's $60M was deliberately split into $14M convertible notes + $46M equity.

**If your investors won't stretch on equity, debt is the pressure valve.**

### Pattern 4 — CCPS, not SAFEs, and no published valuations
**No Indian EV OEM in this set published a SAFE cap, and none of River, Oben, Euler, Tork, Kabira, Hero Electric or Matter disclosed a valuation at signing.** What appears instead is the **CCPS** — compulsory convertible preference shares: Ultraviolette's 5,909 Series C CCPS; Matter's 1,12,837 CCPS at ₹7,320 each; Turno's 18,057 CCPS; Tork's 6,912 CCPS.

Disclosed valuations are mostly *reconstructed from MCA filings afterwards*: Yulu ₹276 cr at Bajaj's Series A → ₹585.2 cr by June 2020; Ola $1B (2019) → $3B (2021). Globally the same pattern holds — Telo's $1.4M seed came with a $10M valuation *stated up front*, which is rare in this space.

### Pattern 5 — What investors actually underwrite: manufacturing cost
*"manufacturing at the right cost."* Note that Lightspeed's Romit Mehta and Speciale Invest's Vishesh Rajaram both said on the record they are **not bullish on entering an OEM as a venture investor at all** at this stage — which is exactly why strategic money dominates the Indian table.

### Pattern 6 — Battery-swap and charging ecosystems attract infra money, not just OEM money
Zeekr's first external cheque came from **Intel Capital and CATL**. Gogoro raised from a **Series B at $20M from Zypp Electric**. For Jedlik — a swappable pod — this is the single most transferable structural insight: **the battery partner is often the first institutional cheque.**

### The cautionary cases — what failure looks like
The global set includes companies that raised enormous amounts and still failed, and these are the most instructive data points in the whole study:

| Company | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Fisker** | ~$1.4B raised from 2007; bankrupt. Dealroom's table shows an early Jan 2008 $20M Series B from Kleiner Perkins and Palo Alto Investors. |
| **e.GO Mobile** | Raised a Series B (closed Feb 2021) yet entered self-administered insolvency in April 2020 after a planned Chinese investment fell through. |
| **Arrival** | $2.1B across 16 rounds; listed via SPAC; later administration. |
| **Faraday Future** | $2.0B at $4.4B valuation from Evergrande — capital without a working manufacturing model. |
| **Lordstown Motors, Mullen, Bollinger, RAD Power, Wrightspeed** | SPAC-era filings and capital raises that did not translate into delivered vehicles. |

**The lesson:** capital amount was never the binding constraint. Manufacturing execution was.

## 4. Government and non-dilutive routes (India)

Verified against the **Startup India Playbook of Government Schemes (June 2026)**, the **PM E-DRIVE gazette of 10 August 2026**, the **DPIIT FoF 2.0 notification of 13 April 2026**, and DST/NIDHI programme portals.

### 4.1 Live for a seed-stage hardware OEM

| Scheme | What you get | Status (late 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **NIDHI PRAYAS 2.0** (DST, not DPIIT) | ₹20 lakh via a PRAYAS Centre; **₹40 lakh via Advanced PRAYAS Centre** (deep-tech) | LIVE, rolling via centres |
| **NIDHI-SSP** (DST) | **Up to ₹1 crore per startup**; ₹10 crore per incubator/TBI. Debt, equity or convertible at the incubator's discretion | LIVE — apply via a supported TBI |
| **NIDHI-Accelerator** (DST) | Up to ₹40 lakh (₹30 lakh to accelerator, ₹10 lakh to startup) | LIVE via supported accelerators |
| **TIDE 2.0 / TIDES** (MeitY) | ₹4 lakh (idea→PoC), ₹7 lakh (prototype), **₹40 lakh** (product development + market outreach) | LIVE via MeitY Startup Hub–listed incubators |
| **CGSS** — Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups | Collateral-free loan guarantee **up to ₹20 crore** per eligible borrower | LIVE — the most relevant debt route for a hardware OEM without property collateral |
| **CGTMSE** | Collateral-free MSE loans; 75% of loan amount guaranteed, cap up to ₹10 crore; enhanced cover for women entrepreneurs and NE units | LIVE, often stacked with CGSS |
| **Startup India FoF 2.0** (DPIIT, April 2026) | ₹10,000 crore corpus into SEBI-registered AIFs investing in deep-tech and manufacturing; SIDBI is implementing agency | LIVE — **indirect route only.** You must get into an FoF-2.0-backed fund's portfolio; you cannot apply directly. Priority sectors explicitly include deep tech + manufacturing |
| **MUDRA / PMMY** | Collateral-free bank term loan up to ₹20–50 lakh | LIVE — adequate for prototypes and tooling, not a vehicle programme or pilot fleet |
| **SIDBI E-Mobility financing** | Direct lending for EVs and EV infrastructure | LIVE — useful for vehicle financing and fleet pilots, typically against assets or receivables |
| **STPI-NGIS** | Up to ₹25 lakh seed/risk + lab, equipment and testing access | LIVE if housed at an STPI — the test-lab access is the real value |
| **SIPP** | Government pays the facilitator's professional fee for patent/trademark filings | LIVE — small money, but file early; it gates grant and PLI eligibility |
| **80-IAC** | 80% deduction on eligible IP expenditure | LIVE — **a deduction, not a cash grant.** Relevant to pod/powertrain R&D |

### 4.2 Live, but only after you have revenue
| Programme | What it is | Why it matters now |
|---|---|---|
| **PM E-DRIVE (e-2W demand incentive)** | ₹5,000 per vehicle FY 2024-25; **₹2,500/kWh capped ₹5,000/vehicle from 01.04.2025 to 31.03.2028**; cap 45,79,120 vehicles; ₹2,767 cr outlay | **Still live** — extended by gazette of 10 Aug 2026 to 31 March 2028 (outlay raised ₹1,190 cr → ₹2,767 cr). But it is a **sale-side reimbursement to a registered sale**, so it is worthless pre-revenue. Use it as a customer-side argument, not a company-side revenue line. Claims must be filed by the OEM within 120 days of sale, once a month. FAME-II-manufactured EVs are not eligible |
| **PM E-DRIVE (charging / swap infrastructure)** | Per-station incentive under MHI charging guidelines | LIVE to 31 Mar 2028. Requires 24×7 uptime and minimum utilisation — swap stations need proven daily throughput to qualify |
| **GeM Startup Runway** | Government procurement access with relaxed prior-experience, turnover and EMD rules | **Potentially high-value and underrated.** Zero/low-emission electric 3-wheelers and fleet vehicles are procured by state transport departments and PSUs. This is a *revenue* route, not capital — and it doubles as a reference customer |
| **PM E-DRIVE (e-3W, e-trucks, e-ambulances)** | Per-kWh, category-capped | Only if Jedlik extends into e-3W/utility |

### 4.3 Closed — do not plan around these
| Programme | Status |
|---|---|
| **SISFS** (up to ₹20 lakh grant + up to ₹50 lakh convertible/debt-linked) | **Closed to new applications 31 May 2026.** Disbursals ongoing. Eligibility required DPIIT recognition, incorporation within 2 years, and **not having received more than ₹10 lakh from any other government scheme** |
| **EMPS 2024** | Closed — superseded by PM E-DRIVE |
| **FAME II** | Closed 31 Mar 2024. Incentives were contingent on using Made-in-India components. **Precedent worth noting:** Hero Electric, a FAME II beneficiary, had to settle a subsidy misappropriation case with the Centre |

### 4.4 Not applicable at pre-seed
- **ACC Battery PLI** (₹18,100 cr outlay, 40 GWh awarded to four firms but only ~1 GWh installed) — a plant-scale manufacturing incentive for gigafactories with capex in the hundreds of crores. **This pays battery makers, not vehicle OEMs.**
- **Automotive Component / Mobility PLI** — investment/milestone-linked, for established plants.

### 4.5 State policies — read the incentive type before valuing it
Two very different things, and confusing them is the most common planning error:

- **Vehicle-side subsidy** (per-unit on sale) → only realisable through registered sales. Near-zero value to a pre-revenue OEM; can be booked as future margin.
- **Capital/interest subsidy + reimbursements + tax/VAT concessions** → realisable post-capex, but only if the plant is in that state.

| State | Vehicle-side e-2W support | Capital-side support | Read for Jedlik |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tamil Nadu** | None directly for e-2W purchase | EV & EV-component manufacturing treated as priority; policy-linked capital subsidy | **Best strategic fit.** The TN auto cluster and SIPCOT matter more than per-vehicle subsidy at this stage |
| **Maharashtra** | ₹9,000 per e-2W (EV Policy 2024) | ₹25,000–₹30,000 per e-vehicle; separate charging-station incentives | Strong fleet-customer density (Mumbai logistics) → commercial advantage; terms depend on local registration |
| **Karnataka** | EV & energy-storage policy | Investment-linked capital incentives | Good if EV supply chain or testing is needed |
| **Gujarat** | e-vehicle purchase subsidy (EV Policy 2022) | Capital subsidy for EV / EV-component manufacturing | Relevant for manufacturing scale-up, not immediate |
| **Delhi** | Historically purchase-subsidy free (deliberate, to push e-buses/commercial EVs) | Mostly n/a | Useful *market* for e-3W/fleet, not for manufacturing |
| **Telangana, AP, UP, Rajasthan, Kerala** | Policy-linked purchase subsidies, typically small and capped | Capital subsidies in some cases | Secondary; verify current guidelines before modelling |

This benefits the *investor*, not the startup — but it is a useful argument in a fundraising meeting, because a fund with Section 194 cover can accept a round more cheaply.

## 5. What this means for Jedlik

Jedlik's position is unusually strong on the dimension investors say decides the round, and unusually exposed on the one they say does not.

**Where you are strong.** A last-mile delivery fleet is a *pre-identified, concentrated, captive customer base*. You are not asking a VC to guess whether commuters will buy a scooter — you are asking how many pods a fleet operator will order and at what unit cost. That converts the chicken-and-egg into a tractable question. Yulu is the closest Indian analogue: it won a strategic investor (Bajaj) precisely because it had fleet demand, not because it had a beautiful vehicle.

**Where you are exposed.** Manufacturing cost, not product features, is what VCs underwrite. For a swappable pod, cost is dominated by the battery and the swap network, not the vehicle. And a swappable model has a structure no pure-play OEM has: **the batteries are the fleet's working capital, not the customer's.** That can be financed separately — and should be. Zeekr (Intel Capital + CATL) and Gogoro (Zypp Electric) both point the way: the battery/tech partner was a first-round investor.

**The realistic raise path, in order.**

1. **Strategic OEM or battery/component partner first, not a seed VC.** Bajaj at Yulu, TVS at Ultraviolette, Bharat Forge at Tork, Al-Abdulla at Kabira, CATL/Intel at Zeekr, Alibaba/Foxconn at XPeng. Your KB's OEM target list (18 ranked OEMs) and Tier 1 India VC list are the right lists — but for this stage the **OEM and the battery/cells partner matter more than the seed fund.** Two questions decide the meeting: what does the pod cost to build at volume, and can you guarantee a fleet volume commitment?
2. **Blend equity with debt.** Oben and IREDA is the template. For a hardware OEM with a manufacturing ramp ahead, IREDA / CGTMSE-backed debt alongside equity is normal, not a weakness signal. CGSS (₹20 crore guarantee) is the route to make that debt collateral-free.
3. **Layer the non-dilutive stack on top.** NIDHI-SSP (up to ₹1 crore) and TIDE 2.0 / PRAYAS 2.0 (₹20–40 lakh) are live and realistic for a pre-revenue hardware prototype; CGSS for the debt leg. Use PM E-DRIVE and GeM Startup Runway as **customer-side and revenue-side** arguments — not as capital. Mind the FAME II sourcing-condition precedent, and treat the 10 Aug 2026 gazette as the controlling instrument over the stale FAQ page.
4. **Consider an accelerator for the uncapped SAFE.** YC's $500K and Techstars' $220K both use an **uncapped MFN SAFE** — no valuation ceiling, so the "we don't publish valuations" problem that every Indian OEM faces disappears. The cost is structural: YC requires US/Canada/Cayman/Singapore incorporation, which for a non-resident Indian company means a corporate flip with the parent owning the IP and assets.
5. **Instrument: expect CCPS, and expect no headline valuation.** Budget for a convertible preference with ratchet/anti-dilution, or a debt-linked instrument with a cap. If a lead wants a published valuation, note that **no comparable Indian EV OEM has given one at seed** — Yulu's ₹276 cr was reconstructed from filings after the fact.
6. **Lead with fleet LOIs, not fleet pilots.** A signed purchase commitment or named fleet customer converts reliability from a promise into a datapoint. Blume's "fast follow" logic means the VC wants to see you copying an innovator already too big — a live fleet deployment, even small, is that evidence.

**What not to do.**
- Do not benchmark against Ola's $3B or a $15M SaaS seed.
- Do not accept a lifeline-only round with no external investor and call it a raise — Bounce's $20M lifeline bought runway, not validation.
- Do not treat ACC Battery PLI as your funding plan — it pays gigafactories, not pod OEMs.
- Do not model state per-vehicle subsidies as near-term revenue for a pre-revenue company.
- Do not lead with the product. Arpit Agarwal's line is the market's line.

---

## 6. The diligence package to assemble before any of this

- DPIIT recognition certificate; certificate of incorporation; Articles; current cap table; founder IP assignment agreements
- Prototype evidence: test data, thermal/load cycles, IP rating, safety validation, BOM with landed cost
- Swap-network model: station capex, units per stall, daily swaps, utilisation ramp, station payback, fleet partner LOIs
- Unit economics per pod: BOM, gross margin at target volume, warranty reserve, battery replacement cost, service cost per km
- Funding plan: 18-month runway, capex schedule, state location decision, which grants apply in which sequence
- Compliance: PM E-DRIVE homologation intent, AIS-136 / IS standards status, recall policy, ISO 9001/14001/45001 roadmap

## 7. Caveats on the research

1. **Tracxn paywalls early-round amounts** behind `****` (rendered as numeric hashes for unauthenticated users); CB Insights gates most rounds behind "Subscribe to see more." Global early-stage amounts therefore come from Dealroom, company announcements, SEC filings and primary press, with Tracxn used for discovery.
2. **Valuation discipline:** disclosed valuations in this space are almost always reconstructed from filings after signing rather than announced at the time. Treat any single valuation number as a floor-mark at best.
3. **All three research streams hit their iteration caps.** The Indian table and the government playbook are complete and primary-source verified. The global table is complete for the companies listed above but does not cover every company targeted (Canoo, Lordstown, Nikola, VinFast, Voltu, PIXmoving, Onvo, Nammi, ElectraMeccanica, NIU, Volta and others were scraped but not normalised). Global early-stage rounds for Canoo, Lordstown, Bollinger, RAD Power, Wrightspeed and Mullen remain to be normalised against primary sources.
4. **The global citation ledger was not re-verified after the last three row corrections.** The table rows still carry their original valid citation IDs, so the mapping remains sound, but a final verification pass should be run before external distribution.
