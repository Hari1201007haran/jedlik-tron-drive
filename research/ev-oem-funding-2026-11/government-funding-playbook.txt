## 4. Standard financing structures in Indian early-stage hardware

### 4.1 SAFE vs CCPS vs convertible note — the Indian reality

**SAFE is not a recognised instrument under Indian corporate law.** There is no Indian SAFE statute. In practice:

- **YC's own deal is papered as a SAFE** — a US Delaware instrument. If Jedlik is a private limited company governed by the Companies Act 2013, a US SAFE is at best an awkward fit: it assumes US concepts (post-money valuation, "company" as a defined term, Delaware law). Indian counsel will normally insist on restating it.
- **Practical Indian workaround: a CCPS or a convertible instrument** (subscription agreement + shareholders agreement, or an interest-bearing convertible instrument). Indian funds frequently sign a paper described as a "SAFE" but the executed agreement is a **CCPS** or a **convertible instrument** with a conversion trigger. Confirm the executed document, not the label.
- **Enforceability**: a bare SAFE or CCPS without a shareholders' agreement has weak enforcement in India. Indian counsel must place the conversion mechanics, information rights, protective covenants, and a **long-stop date** in the subscription agreement, because a CCPS alone does not give the investor board/inspection rights. Clause referencing an "instrument" under a non-existent Indian SAFE regime creates risk.
- **Recommendation:** paper the round as **CCPS + SHA** (clean, litigable, understood by Indian funds and tax authorities) rather than importing a US SAFE. If a global investor insists on SAFE paper, negotiate a side letter with explicit conversion to CCPS on the next priced round, plus an interim deemed-conversion percentage.

### 4.2 Instrument comparison

| Instrument | Use when | Pros | Cons / Indian cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CCPS (convertible redeemable cumulative preferred shares)** | Standard Indian seed instrument; convertible on a priced round | Native to Indian law; tax-efficient; understood by Indian VCs and founders' lawyers; survives the SEBI/private-placement constraints because the company remains private | Requires careful SHA: conversion ratio, price cap/floor, anti-dilution (broad-based weighted average), liquidation preference (1x non-participating is the Indian norm), ESOP pool treatment, information rights, drag/tag, founder vesting |
| **Convertible instrument / convertible note (unsecured, interest-bearing)** | Bridge or fast close when CCPS mechanics are too slow | Quick to sign; no share capital formalities; easy to unwind | Unsecured claim ranks behind secured debt; accruing interest and a hard conversion date create founder risk; if the company has a Series A bridge deadline and misses it, repayment can trigger a liquidity crisis. Noting: a note with a fixed conversion ratio can breach the issue-price/pro forma rules — counsel must check |
| **SAFE** | Only if the investor requires US paper | Fast, founder-friendly, no interest, no maturity date | **Not an Indian statutory instrument.** Weak enforcement; must be backed by a SHA and ideally restated as CCPS at conversion. Cap table modelling must use post-money semantics carefully |
| **RFA / revenue-linked or loan** | Funding working capital or a paid pilot against receivables | Non-dilutive; predictable | Senior to equity in a wind-down; cash-flow repayment risk; not appropriate for early-stage with no revenue |
| **Direct equity (priced round)** | Seed / Series A when valuation and syndicate are settled | Simple, clean, certain | Immediate dilution; slower |
| **Warrants / sweat equity** | Small top-ups or founder incentives | Low cash cost | Accounting and ESOP-limit complexity |

### 4.3 Real Indian precedents

- **Ather Energy** (Indian EV OEM) — the relevant benchmark. It has used **convertible warrants** alongside equity in its listed-company funding rounds, and **rights issues/warrant structures** to fund scale-up. This shows that Indian EV companies use convertible paper rather than US SAFEs at scale, and that listed-EV comparables fund through structured equity, not grants. ([Ather investor relations](https://www.atherenergy.com/investor-relations))
- **Oxygen Biotech** and comparable Indian deep-tech startups — issue **CCPS with a shareholders' agreement**; the de facto market standard. ([IISc incubation / startup India case material](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/))
- **YS-Business and other YC India alumni** — YC's SAFE is commonly restructured on the Indian side into CCPS + SHA by Indian counsel; investors accept this, usually in exchange for the YC terms being replicated in the SHA (MFN, pro-rata, information rights).

### 4.4 Dilution and valuation norms (Indian pre-seed → seed → Series A)

Typical Indian benchmarks (contextual; verify against current deal data):

| Round | Typical cheque | Post-money valuation | Dilution accepted | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-seed / angel** | ₹25L–₹2cr | ₹5cr–₹25cr | 10–20% | Often SAFE/CCPS; founder-heavy, angels and founder networks |
| **Seed** | ₹2cr–₹15cr | ₹25cr–₹150cr | 15–25% | Priced equity plus CCPS clean-up; DFIs and seed funds enter |
| **Series A** | ₹20cr–₹100cr | ₹150cr–₹600cr+ | 15–25% | Real revenue, fleet contracts and unit economics required |

**Key norms:**
- **ESOP pool:** typically **10–15% at pre-seed/seed**, created at Series A often at 10–12% (some teams 15–20% for deep-tech/hardware). In India, the post-IPO ceiling of **10%** (15% with promoter/sponsor conditions) constrains later top-ups, so pre-IPO founders should pre-create the pool above the future requirement. ([DPIIT/Startup India guidance on ESOP](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/))
- **Dilution:** budget **~45–60% total dilution across pre-seed → Series A**, not per round. This is the number that actually matters — per-round 15–20% compounds to45–65%.
- **Founder ownership:** a Series A company typically has founders at 35–55%, with ESOP 10–15%, investors 30–45%, angels/incubators 5–10%.
- **Valuation discipline:** price the *pod and swap network economics*, not the "EV" narrative. Investors will want per-pod gross margin, swap-station capex and payback, fleet churn, and cost per km.

## 5. Prioritised application checklist for Jedlik

**Tier 1 — apply now (0–3 months)**
1. **NIDHI Seed Support Programme (SSP)** via a NIDHI TBI. Up to ₹1cr. Highest-value direct route. Build the TBI-relationship first.
2. **NIDHI-PRAYAS 2.0** via an Advanced PRAYAS Centre (₹40L) for powertrain/pod prototype.
3. **IIT Madras CoE / Advanced PRAYAS Centre / TIDES (DSSS)** — local, Anna University adjacency, and the CoE call calendar is the single most important recurring source of non-dilutive money and lab access. Check the IIT Madras CoE and PRAYAS Centre call pages monthly.
4. **DPIIT recognition + 80-IAC + SIPP** — file now. Every grant route keys off DPIIT recognition; 80-IAC and SIPP reduce cash burn.
5. **CGSS via a participating lender** — open a relationship with a bank/NBFC that offers collateral-free startup credit under CGSS. Apply on the strength of the grant pipeline, not just current revenue.

**Tier 2 — build toward (3–9 months)**
6. **PM E-DRIVE e-2W registration** — plan the GIA/manufacturer-registration route now so that commercial sales from FY2027-28 attract ₹2,500/kWh (max ₹5,000/vehicle). Model it as margin, not capital.
7. **PM E-DRIVE charging / swapping infrastructure** — only after the first stations have real utilisation; reimbursement-based and capped.
8. **GeM Startup Runway** — register as a seller; pursue state transport department and PSU e-3W/fleet tenders. This is revenue, and revenue is what unlocks the next round.
9. **State capital subsidy** — decide Tamil Nadu (cluster + capital subsidy) versus Maharashtra (fleet density + per-vehicle subsidy) based on where the first plant and first fleet customers are. Do not chase both.
10. **FoF 2.0 / FFS** — do not apply directly. Get into a SEBI-registered AIF whose mandate covers manufacturing/deep tech, and present as a domestic-capital anchor.

**Tier 3 — capital events (6–18 months)**
11. **Y Combinator or Techstars** — both now give an uncapped MFN SAFE structure, which is unusually founder-friendly. Expect ~1–2% acceptance at YC and a heavier equity/SAFE load (7% and 5% respectively). Apply when there is a prototype, a working swap demo, and two or three named fleet customers.
12. **Proparco / IFC** — engage only when manufacturing or fleet-financing scale is visible; not a pre-seed equity cheque.
13. **Corporate programmes (Hyundai Cradle, Porsche Startup Garage, Toyota Ventures, Dacia/Accelerate, Up! Lyon)** — pursue for pilots, certifications, and customer logos; do not model them as funding.

**Explicitly skip:** ACC battery PLI and Automotive Component PLI (plant-scale, incompatible with pre-seed), BIRAC (biotech), FAME II and EMPS 2024 (closed), EBRD (not an India DFI), and the old SISFS application (closed).

## 6. Diligence package to assemble before applying

- DPIIT recognition certificate; certificate of incorporation; Articles; current cap table; founder IP assignment agreements
- Prototype evidence: test data, thermal/load cycles, IP rating, safety validation, BOM with landed cost
- Swap-network model: station capex, units/stall, daily swaps, utilisation ramp, station payback, fleet partner LOIs
